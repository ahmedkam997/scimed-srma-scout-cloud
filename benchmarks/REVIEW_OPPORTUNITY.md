# Update & corrective discovery v1

Sources: Cochrane Handbook IV (https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-iv), Garner et al. BMJ 2016 i3507 (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i3507), AHRQ Ottawa/RAND surveillance methods (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK148795/), RobotReviewer LIVE pilot (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2022.08.013), AMSTAR 2 (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j4008), ROBIS (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26092286/). We adapt the decision principles, not their validated methods or tools.

The engine identifies source-linked opportunities, does not estimate publication probability or declare a review erroneous. Existing novelty, source coverage, trial-family and feasibility gates remain in place. Related reviews must have strong source-linked semantic overlap cues; all material reviews need traceable search date excerpts/URLs. Use interval chronology; do not substitute publication date for the review cutoff. Separate later reports on known earlier trial families, unlinked reports, uncertain cases and registry-linked family signals. No signal is not proof of no new evidence. A correction cue is a prompt to inspect methods, not an AMSTAR/ROBIS rating or a verified critical flaw. A search restriction or single reviewer may be contextual and requires full-text assessment. A dedicated provisional route retains opportunities and prioritizes the existing follow-up queue; no automatic GO. Older saved reports are not retrospectively rescored by this change.

Tests are deterministic synthetic regression fixtures, not externally validated clinical decisions. The next stage should independently adjudicate real historical update and correction examples, latest search dates, included-trial lists, impact on results and false positive/negative rates. No extra AI requests, dependencies or paid services.
